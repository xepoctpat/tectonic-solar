What each usually means
Subduction zones: trenches, volcanic arcs, compression, and deeper Benioff-zone earthquakes
Collision and oceanic convergence: crustal shortening, uplift, and stronger compressional belts
Divergent zones: ridges and rifts, crust spreading, shallower extensional earthquakes
Transform zones: strike-slip motion, shallow earthquakes, and lateral plate shear
Most global earthquake geography is explained first by plate interaction, even before any space-weather hypothesis is considered.

ⓘ Map reading guide

Plate regions show the cited PB2002 present-day plate model. They are context, not live deformation measurements.

Boundary families describe relative plate interaction: red = convergent, cyan/green = divergent, amber = transform.

Earthquake markers are the active layer: they sit above the static context, pulse gently, and grow with magnitude. Color represents magnitude; border thickness represents depth.

Motion arrows are Bird (2003) Euler-pole-derived reference-frame vectors, not real-time GPS velocities.

Volcanic eruptions are not shown yet; the map does not claim a live volcano-event feed.

Use the Controls button to toggle layers, basemaps, regions, and the magnitude filter.

Space Weather – Earthquake Correlation

Current window, quick evidence summary, and the live 30-day storm-versus-seismic timeline.

Research Background

Some peer-reviewed studies report a statistically significant correlation between geomagnetic storms and seismic activity with a 27–28 day lag — approximately one synodic solar rotation period.

The proposed mechanism involves tidal or electromagnetic stress loading on fault zones during the elevated geomagnetic environment following a CME impact.

Odintsov et al. (2006); Han et al. (2004); Rabeh et al. (2010);
Varga & Grafarend (2018); AGU 2025 poster session.

⚠️ USGS and most seismologists state no proven causal relationship exists. This tool is for research and pattern exploration only — not earthquake prediction.

Active Correlation Window

Any geomagnetic storm (Kp≥5) occurring 27–28 days ago falls within the proposed elevated-risk window for M5+ seismic events.

About This Application

Real-time monitoring of space weather, global seismic activity, and correlation analysis — in a no-build browser UI, with the local Node proxy providing full live-data support.

Live Data Sources

  • USGS Earthquake Catalog — M4.5+ real-time GeoJSON
  • NOAA SWPC — Solar wind, Kp index, X-ray flux
  • Open-Meteo — Weather & air quality (no API key)
  • Peter Bird PB2002 plate model — curated local GeoJSON artifacts for the map's plate regions and global boundary layer

Features

  • Live solar wind plasma & Kp index from NOAA DSCOVR/ACE
  • Real Kp 3-day history chart with storm threshold line
  • Real solar wind speed history chart
  • Live USGS earthquake list with depth encoding
  • Magnitude filter slider on map
  • Real weather + air quality via Open-Meteo (free)
  • Browser & in-app toast notifications
  • 27–28 day geomagnetic ↔ seismic correlation timeline
  • Cited PB2002 plate regions, subtype-styled tectonic boundaries & computed plate-motion vectors for all 52 plates (Bird 2003 Euler poles, Pacific reference frame)
  • Settings persistence via localStorage
